FALCON EYE: Independent Collision-Awareness Avionics

A fog-honest, edge-accelerated safety payload fusing LiDAR and radar. Validated through 2,400 Monte Carlo simulation trials, with a 10-core accelerator in RTL design and cycle-accurate simulation.

Beer-Lambert Fog Physics

Optical attenuation modeling (I = I0 · e^(−αd)). In our dense-fog scenario (100 m visibility, extinction about 0.039 per metre) the two-way LiDAR return collapses beyond roughly 100 m, so simulated detection falls from 100% in clear air to 0%.

Sensor Fusion Engine

Confidence-weighted fusion of LiDAR and radar measurements feeding an alpha-beta tracker. 100% detection across every simulated fog level. Known limitation: single-hit fusion raises false alarms (see Charts).

10-Core RTL TPU Hardware

10-core accelerator with 8x8 systolic arrays and ping-pong memory, now in RTL design. In cycle-accurate simulation, an optimized scheduler cut missed emergency deadlines under memory-bus pressure from up to 47 of 480 to zero.

Governing Equations & Dynamic Weighting

Beer-Lambert Extinction Law:
I(d) = I_0 \cdot \exp(-\alpha_{fog} \cdot d)

Extinction scales with fog density (derived from meteorological visibility). In dense fog (100 m visibility) the LiDAR return collapses at range, while radar is modeled as nearly fog-insensitive.

Confidence-Weighted Fusion:
w_i = c_i / (c_LiDAR + c_Radar), fused = Σ w_i · measurement_i

Each sensor's measurement is weighted by its confidence, which falls for LiDAR as fog thickens, so the fused estimate leans on radar.

Detection Rate vs. Fog Density

Reading the Chart

What the data shows:

As shown in this graph, single-sensor LiDAR (blue line) collapses from 100% detection in clear air down to 0% in dense fog due to atmospheric optical scattering.

In contrast, Falcon Eye Fusion (green line) maintains a perfect 100% detection rate across all weather regimes because radar is modeled as nearly fog-insensitive (to be validated on real hardware).

System Availability vs. Fog Density

Reading the Chart

What the data shows:

Availability measures tracking continuity. LiDAR availability drops below 20% in moderate fog and vanishes completely in heavy fog.

In simulation, Falcon Eye held 1.0 (100%) tracking availability across every fog level simulated, avoiding the cliff LiDAR alone shows.

False Alarm Rate: Radar vs. Current Fusion

Known Limitation

What the data shows:

Our first fusion logic accepts a detection from either sensor, and in fog that raised false alarms.

In dense fog the false-alarm rate was 89% for fusion versus 18% for radar alone. Track confirmation, requiring agreement between sensors, is our next fusion upgrade.
